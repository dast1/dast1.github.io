I have put capital to work, alongside building software, for about a decade. It has been personal and family money: real estate, hotels, multifamily housing, private businesses, public equities, technology, and alternatives. The part worth writing down is the change in what I notice. What a system is allowed to decide, and what it is allowed to do, is a technical question, and it is not this one. This note is about the organization, and the owner, that has to live with the answer.

Operators watch throughput. Owners get stuck on what remains when throughput looks fine and the thing is still wrong. Deferred maintenance. A manager who is good in the meeting and careless in the building. A report that is accurate and aimed at the wrong decision. Incentives that pay for motion.

AI is showing up inside companies first as a throughput tool. Draft faster, search faster, answer faster. That will matter, and it is the easy part to buy. It is also the easy part to copy. Once ten analyses can exist before lunch, the scarce object is no longer the memo. It is knowing which memo is allowed to move money, move a team, or change how a property is run. The shift that matters next is from systems that answer to systems that act. Acting does not reduce the scarcity. It raises the cost of being casual about who may commit.

Organizations will get redesigned around that scarcity, or they will drown in fluent paper. Fewer layers whose job is to carry information upward. Clearer rights about who may commit. Tools that leave the context of a decision inspectable, so a person can actually be responsible for it. An organization that has taken the tools seriously has separated preparation from judgment, and has been honest about which work is which.

Investing and building meet in the same three questions. What information is in the room. Who is accountable for the call. What happens if the call is wrong. I am interested in systems that make those questions harder to fake: when software can act and not only draft, and when a person is committing capital.
